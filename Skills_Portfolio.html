Reflection on Professional Conduct

During this module, I’ve been able to think about not only the academic work I’ve completed but also how I’ve presented myself as someone growing in my professional journey. This year has been tough, and when I think about it, I can see how much I’ve developed—not just in my abilities, but also in my resilience, responsibility, and understanding of myself. Juggling a full-time warehouse job with night shifts while also studying full-time at university has really challenged my professionalism in ways I didn't see coming. There were so many times when I would wrap up work at sunrise, rush home to get ready, and then head straight to class with barely any sleep. Even though I was really tired, I focused on going to my lectures, finishing my assignments on time, and participating in seminars. I always aimed to keep my job from being a reason to lag behind, so I focused on managing my time wisely, giving up some personal time to stay on top of all my responsibilities. This shows how dedicated I am, how disciplined I can be, and my determination to meet my responsibilities, even when things get tough. This year, the most significant moment for me happened during the Christmas break when I faced the heartbreaking loss of a close family member. This loss really affected me and caught me off guard, leaving me feeling really overwhelmed with sadness. During the weeks that followed, I found it really hard to stay motivated or even muster the emotional energy to go to classes or concentrate on my assignments. I missed a few sessions during that period, not because I didn’t care, but because I just wasn’t emotionally prepared to continue as normal. It seemed as if everything around me had come to a standstill. Reflecting on this, I see that it was a significant moment in my understanding of professionalism. Initially, I was really anxious and concerned that not attending classes would make me look bad. As I thought about it, I realised that being professional isn't only about meeting deadlines or showing up all the time. It's also about knowing when to ask for help, being truthful about what you're going through, and taking care of yourself when things get tough. I reached out to my tutors via email to explain the situation and ask for their understanding and support. I learnt from their compassion that being professional can—and should—mean being empathetic, flexible, and human. This experience taught me that being professional is about how we deal with challenges, not just our achievements. Although I couldn't fully participate during those weeks, I really tried to catch up on the work I missed as soon as I felt emotionally prepared. I relied on my time management skills, utilised the resources I had, and slowly got myself back on track. I now understand resilience—not just getting through tough times, but also adapting, bouncing back, and moving forward—as an essential aspect of how we should act in our careers. Studying the BCS Code of Conduct really helped me link these lessons to the broader professional environment. I really noticed how important integrity, responsibility, respect, and competence are. They aren't just rules; they're qualities I want to bring with me into my future career. I’ve begun to incorporate these principles in little ways, like respecting copyright in my design projects, being aware of diversity and inclusion, and feeling proud of producing work that’s ethically responsible and considerate. I’ve realised that a big part of growing professionally is recognising my mistakes or weaknesses and putting in the effort to get better. I also see the parts where I still have room to grow. I really want to build my confidence when it comes to professional interactions, particularly in situations such as networking events, client meetings, or when I'm presenting my work. I occasionally find it hard to voice my opinions or stand up for myself, and I realise that developing this confidence will be crucial for my future career. I want to create a more consistent professional presence online by using platforms such as LinkedIn and Behance to share my work, connect with others, and demonstrate my dedication to continuous learning. I've come to realise that professionalism isn't just about meeting certain standards—it's a continuous effort to behave with integrity, respect, accountability, and empathy, both for others and for myself. This year has really highlighted for me the importance of balancing ambition with taking care of myself, and being strong while also staying humble. I believe that the lessons I learn will stick with me even after this module is over, influencing the type of creative professional—and individual—I want to be.
